Biomechatronics Hand Exoskeletons

Wearable orthotic devices designed to enhance and restore upper-limb mobility.

Why

Designed to assist individuals with minimal grip strength by shifting mechanical loads away from the hand through automated linkages.

How

Iterative 3D printing for structural optimization. The EMG version uses an ESP32 to process neuromuscular signals and trigger movement.

Specs

100% 3D-printed and fully scalable. Features tool-less adjustability and intuitive signal-based actuation.

Prototyping & Signal Integration

The development of this exoskeleton was a lesson in rapid iteration. As seen in the gallery, the design went through dozens of physical revisions to find the "sweet spot" between structural rigidity and lightweight wearability.

The most significant technical hurdle was the integration of Electromyography (EMG) sensors. By placing sensors on the forearm, the system detects micro-electrical signals sent from the brain to the muscles. An ESP32 microcontroller filters this "noise" and translates intent into servo-driven motion, effectively amplifying the user's natural grip strength without requiring high physical effort.
