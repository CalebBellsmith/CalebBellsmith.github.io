How this works

The moving picture is 12,288 tracer particles carried by a lattice-Boltzmann solver running on your GPU over the waterplane, with the hull's waterline at this load as the obstacle. It runs at a scaled-down Reynolds number, so it shows the character of the flow (the wake, separation, eddies) rather than full-scale numbers. The numbers come from the hull itself: ITTC-57 friction on the wetted surface, a form factor from full-scale OpenFOAM runs, Michell's thin-ship integral for wave drag, and slender-body theory for side force. The wave pattern is the Kelvin pattern for this speed, scaled for visibility.

How this works

An explicit finite-element shell model of the hull (about 1,000 nodes) runs in your browser. Each element carries its real layup: 6 oz glass outside and in, 6.35 mm cedar between, an extra glass layer on the bottom, ash gunwales and cherry stems. Paddlers and gear are soft masses coupled to the bottom near the seats, and the surrounding water adds mass. The colour is a failure index, stress divided by strength, worked out for every ply. Green is comfortable, orange is getting close, and red (1 or more) means that ply would fail. "Structure" covers the glass, cedar along the grain, shear, gunwales and stems. "Core cracks" is cedar splitting across the grain inside the glass, the usual first sign of a hard knock. Deformation is real but exaggerated so you can see it.
